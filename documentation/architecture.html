00

What we found

Before proposing anything, we audited what Soulfables already is. The brief describes a platform to build; the live site describes a product that already exists and is further along than the brief admits.

soulfables.co currently 301-redirects to soulfables.base44.app. The House is running on Base44, an AI app-builder. That single fact explains most of the brief: why §1 insists on "a real software product, not a no-code prototype," why §26 is unusually specific about ownership, and why §23 notes that delivery problems have already happened.

What the audit turned up matters, because it changes what this project is:

  • 32 stories across 27 shelves, four authors — including The Librarian, which is a House persona rather than a person.
  • Shelves are not categories. They are emotional entry points with their own copy, a curated first read, and a Librarian's note that deliberately sends readers elsewhere. The shelf labelled "Sleepless" lives at /shelf/anxiety — label, URL and theme are already three different things.
  • The journey graph already exists. Each shelf page states where readers arrive from and where they continue to. Today that is editorial judgement. It is also, structurally, the Soulfables Intelligence Platform from §14 — already designed, just not yet computed.
  • The shop sells five things, not one. An ebook at $7.99, an anthology at $16, a journal at $18, a reflection deck at $15, and a $45 bundle containing everything. The brief describes a single-ebook system; the reality needs bundles that fan out into multiple entitlements.
  • /wander and /residents return 404 while both sit in the main navigation.
  • The footer exposes a "Studio" column — Roadmap, Design System, UI Kit — internal tooling leaking into the public site.

The consequence

This is not a greenfield build. It is a migration with live customers, existing content, and indexed URLs — and the brief is silent on all three. Cutover is scoped in §06 rather than discovered during launch week.

What the brief gets right

Worth stating plainly, because it shapes how much of this document is correction versus confirmation: §22's rule that a frontend success message is never proof of payment, §23's entitlement chain, §11's private-by-default journal, §26's ownership clause, and the instinct to treat architecture as the first deliverable are all correct and are carried through unchanged.

01

Decisions required

Five questions only Soulfables can answer. Two of them block the build; the rest can be deferred but get more expensive the later they are made.

D1

How does Soulfables take money?

Blocks Phase 3

Stripe does not support businesses registered in Rwanda. The brief names Stripe with "subject to availability" attached, but this is the largest practical constraint in the project and it is a legal and tax decision before it is a technical one. Three viable paths:

  • Merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) — they become the legal seller, absorbing both the entity problem and EU VAT on digital goods, which the brief never mentions. Higher fees, far less administration.
  • African payment infrastructure (Flutterwave, Paystack) — supports RWF and MTN Mobile Money, which matters if a meaningful share of readers are East African.
  • Stripe through a foreign entity (US LLC, UK Ltd) — unlocks Stripe, but adds company formation, accounting, and cross-border tax obligations.

Recommendation: a merchant of record for global card payments, with an African PSP added in Phase 3.5 if local sales justify it. The audience for a Rwandan author writing in English is likely split between diaspora and local readers, and those two groups pay by different rails.

D2

What carries over from Base44?

Blocks Phase 2

Existing buyers, existing orders, 32 story bodies, and the current URL structure. If any Soulfables page ranks in search today, changing URLs without 301 redirects loses that ranking permanently.

Recommendation: preserve every existing URL shape (/shelf/:slug, /stories, /shop), export story bodies to MDX, and import prior customers with entitlement rows so their books appear in the new library on day one. If the honest answer is "nothing worth carrying over," that is a fine answer — but it should be written down rather than assumed.

D3

What is the budget ceiling?

Scopes everything

The brief asks for a cost estimate across 30 sections without naming a range. No developer can scope that honestly, and quotes received against it will not be comparable.

Recommendation: set a figure for Phases 1–3 only and treat later phases as separately funded. §07 gives the ranges to choose from.

D4

May the AI companion read journal entries?

Design constraint

"Private by default" (§11) and "an AI that helps users journal" (§13) quietly contradict each other. This needs an explicit answer, because it is very hard to walk back after readers have trusted the journal with real material.

Recommendation: no by default, opt-in per entry. The schema already carries both a global setting and a per-entry flag, and the AI service reads only where both are true.

D5

Flutter, or share code with the web?

Phase 5

The brief's own folder diagram implies packages/design-system and packages/models are shared across apps. They cannot be: those are TypeScript, and Flutter is Dart. Mobile will duplicate both.

Recommendation: keep Flutter — a reader with offline audio is exactly what it is good at — but generate Dart types from the database schema so drift is caught by CI rather than by readers. Choose React Native instead only if code sharing matters more than native reading polish.

Deviation 1 — admin ships inside the web app

The brief specifies apps/admin as a separate application. For launch, admin is a protected route group inside apps/web instead: one deployment, one auth session, one RLS surface, roughly half the configuration. The folder shape in §4 of the brief remains the destination, not the starting commit. Splitting it out later is a mechanical change; maintaining two apps from week one is a permanent tax paid for a benefit that arrives much later.

Deviation 2 — the monorepo starts smaller

Workspace packages exist from the start (design-system, models, shared) but ship TypeScript source consumed directly by Next, with no build step of their own. Publishing pipelines get added when a second consumer genuinely needs them.

03

Database schema

Twelve migrations, roughly fifty tables. Three decisions in it are worth defending, because they are what let the MVP stay small without foreclosing §14.

Shelves and themes are different things

The live site already proves they must be. A theme is flat editorial classification used for filters and SEO. A shelf is a place in the House with a title, a tagline, a Librarian's note, a curated entry story, and a position in the journey graph. Modelling them as one table would force a rename to break a URL, or a re-tag of every story to change a shelf's copy.

The journey graph is rows, not prose

"Readers usually come here from Grief, Love, Heartbreak — and usually continue to Healing, Hope, Forgiveness" is currently editorial instinct. Storing those edges as rows with a weight and an is_editorial flag costs almost nothing now, and means the same interface can later be driven by observed behaviour without a redesign. The recommendation system in §15 becomes a scheduled job that recomputes weights, not a new feature.

shelf_journeys
  shelf_id  →  related_shelf_id
  direction     arrives_from | continues_to
  weight        0.0000 – 1.0000
  is_editorial  true today, false once computed

Entitlements are the only source of access

This is the answer to §23, and it is one sentence: access to a file is never derived from an order, a session, a redirect, or anything the browser said — only from a row in entitlements, written by a verified webhook.

Everything around it exists to make that rule survivable. Products have many files (epub, pdf, future formats). Bundles fan out at grant time, so buying the $45 library produces an entitlement per contained product. Refunds revoke. A partial unique index permits re-purchase after revocation. Webhook events are logged with a unique provider event id, so a provider retry storm cannot double-grant.

The failure this prevents

If step 5 never arrives, the customer sees "we're confirming your order" and an alert fires — they do not see a success screen with no book behind it. The failure mode of every bug in this chain is a customer who has to wait, never a stranger who gets the file.

Guest checkout

Orders may be placed without an account and are claimed when someone later signs up with the same email, at which point entitlements are granted retroactively. This also handles importing prior Base44 customers: create paid orders against their email addresses, and their library assembles itself when they sign in.

Other flows specified in full

  • Account — register, verify, sign in, reset, delete (with full data export).
  • Reading — open, progress autosaved, resume across devices, complete, prompted to reflect.
  • Journal — daily question, mood, optional story link, private save, private search.
  • Premium — listing and blurb public for SEO; body never sent to the browser unless access passes.

Development roadmap

The brief's seven phases, resequenced once. Phase 3 moves ahead of Phase 2, because selling reliably is the problem Soulfables actually has today.

P13–4 weeks

Foundation

Repo, CI, Supabase, schema, RLS, auth, design system, admin shell, content operations. Ends with Soulfables able to write and publish a story unaided.

P24–5 weeks

Bookshop & delivery

Products, checkout, webhook, entitlements, signed downloads, My Library, receipts. Includes importing prior customers. This is the revenue fix.

P34–5 weeks

Library & reader

Content migration from Base44 with 301s, shelves, journey graph, reader, search, saves, bookmarks. Public launch at the end of this phase.

P42–3 weeks

Journal & Letter

Private journal, prompts, moods, story-linked reflections, Letter archive and double opt-in. Wander ships here.

P58–12 weeks

Mobile

Flutter for iOS and Android: auth, library, reader, journal, offline audio. Longest phase; entirely optional at launch.

P64–6 weeks

Intelligence

Embeddings, content-based recommendations, computed journey weights, then the AI companion with its safety layer.

P74–6 weeks

Residents

Community and membership, once the concept is decided. Deliberately last.

Launchable scope

Phases 1–3, roughly 11–14 weeks for one senior full-stack developer. Everything after that is a funded extension, not a prerequisite. Keep the Base44 site live and selling throughout — revenue should not go dark for a rebuild.

M2 comes early on purpose

The riskiest part of this project is the payment-to-download chain, and it has already failed once. Proving it end-to-end in week five — before the library, before the design polish — is worth more than any other sequencing choice in this roadmap.

09

Security plan

Authorisation lives in the database. Application code is a convenience layer over it, never the thing standing between a stranger and a paid file.

Row Level Security, deny by default

RLS is enabled on every table. A table with no policy is reachable only by the service role, which exists solely in server-side code and is never shipped to a browser or a phone. Two exceptions are deliberate and worth stating out loud:

  • Staff cannot read journal entries. No policy grants it. An administrator can see that a reader has fourteen entries; never what any of them says. Deletion requests are actioned by a logged script, not by browsing a dashboard.
  • No client role can write entitlements. There is no insert, update, or delete policy for any client on that table. Access is granted only by the service role, only after a signature-verified webhook.

Paid files

Every EPUB and PDF lives in a private bucket with no read policy for anyone — including the customer who owns the book. Downloads are served exclusively as short-lived signed URLs minted server-side after has_entitlement() passes, and every mint is logged with IP and user agent. There is no public bucket into which a paid file could be uploaded by mistake.

Payment integrity

  • Amounts are computed server-side from the database. A tampered client price is ignored.
  • Webhook signatures are verified before the payload is read.
  • Event ids are unique-indexed, making replays and retry storms no-ops.
  • paid_at is writable only by the webhook handler.

Also covered

Rate limiting on auth, checkout, download, and AI endpoints. Zod validation at every boundary. Secrets in the platform secret store, never in a repository or on a developer's machine alone. Audit logging on every admin action, append-only, with no update or delete policy for any role including owner. Dependency scanning in CI. Quarterly access review.

Safety, which is a security concern here

A library about heartbreak, grief and healing, paired with an emotional AI companion, will encounter readers in crisis. "Not a therapist" (§13) is necessary and nowhere near sufficient. The plan is: a distress classifier on companion input, routing to region-appropriate crisis resources held in a table the House can correct without a deploy, no diagnostic language, flagged turns retained for review, and the journal consent rule from D4 enforced in the query itself rather than in a prompt.

Pipeline

Every pull request runs typecheck, lint, unit and integration tests, an RLS coverage check that fails the build if a new table has no policy, and a preview deployment. Merges to main deploy to staging automatically; production releases are tagged and manually promoted. Database migrations are forward-only, numbered, reviewed as code, and applied before the deployment that depends on them.

Cutover from Base44

  1. Freeze content edits on Base44.
  2. Export stories to MDX; import with slugs preserved exactly.
  3. Import prior customers as paid orders with entitlements.
  4. Verify all 32 stories and the full checkout chain on staging against the production database.
  5. Publish the 301 map; test every legacy URL.
  6. Cut DNS during the lowest-traffic window, with the Base44 deployment retained as an immediate rollback.
  7. Email prior customers that their library is ready.
  8. Watch error rates and webhook success for 48 hours before declaring completion.

Backup and recovery

Daily automated Postgres backups with point-in-time recovery to seven days, plus a weekly off-platform export so recovery does not depend on a single vendor account remaining accessible. Storage buckets versioned. Target recovery point one hour, recovery time four hours. Restores are rehearsed quarterly — an untested backup is a belief, not a backup.

Ownership checklist

§26 of the brief is a promise being asked of a developer. It works far better as a set of accounts that already exist.

Create every one of these under a Soulfables address before a developer is engaged, then invite them in with the access they need. Ownership you create is ownership you hold; ownership you request is a clause you have to enforce later, usually at the worst moment.

One line to add to §30

"No capability may be added that cannot be operated by Soulfables through the admin dashboard or a documented runbook." That sentence prevents developer lock-in more effectively than the entire ownership list, because it constrains the design rather than the paperwork.
